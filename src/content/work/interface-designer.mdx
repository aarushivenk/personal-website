## The problem

Interface Designer is where Appian developers build the screens their own users
see. It's a drag-and-drop editor that had barely changed in a decade. Experts
could make something beautiful in it. Everyone else made something functional and
stopped there.

  tint="lilac"

  caption="Interface Designer before the redesign"

My PM and I talked to developers, studied competitors, and built in the tool
ourselves. Three things kept coming up: it was slow, the settings were buried in
long nested lists, and it looked dated next to the tools developers used
everywhere else. Most people avoided the no-code controls entirely and wrote code
instead.

<Callout label="Key insight">
  Before a group can agree on a design, it has to agree on what the design should
  make people feel.
</Callout>

## What I did

The editor had over 300 configuration settings, all in one long scrolling list.
Card sorting showed clear categories, but the team was split: group them into
tabs (more clicks) or keep one list (more scrolling)? A/B testing came back
mixed, and we didn't have time to build both. So I walked the team through real
user flows, and tabs won. Our content writer and I then placed every one of the
300 settings into a category. In usability testing, one developer said they used
the tabs several times without even realizing they were new.

### Thinking past the panel

Tabs versus one long list wasn't the only decision on the table. Each change to Interface Designer raised a bigger question: what should building in Appian feel like a few years from now, and where should the platform grow next?
So we sketched that future first: an editor that's AI-first, with intuitive visual controls and fast performance. Then we worked backward into a release plan. That vision decided what shipped first and what waited, so each release moved us toward it instead of just solving today's problems.

## Outcome

The new Interface Designer launched at Appian World 2025, and engineering made it
dramatically faster at the same time. One solutions engineer told us they used to
keep it out of customer demos and now they're comfortable showing it.

## What I learned

We made Interface Designer faster and easier to use, but the definition of a modern tool shifted under us as AI changed what developers expect. The lesson I took: design for where the product is going, and plan to keep revisiting it.
